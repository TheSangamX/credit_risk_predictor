The model flags applicants at 15% or more, a line chosen to catch more real defaulters than the default 50% would.

What moved the estimate

    Model card

    scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingClassifier (200 iterations) trained on the Home Credit Default Risk application data. Its trees and preprocessing were exported from the saved pipeline to JSON and are evaluated here in JavaScript; predictions match scikit-learn to within 1e-6.

    Metric (test set)Score
    ROC-AUC0.760
    Recall at the 15% threshold41.8%
    Precision at the 15% threshold25.0%
    F1 at the 15% threshold31.3%
    Accuracy85.2%

    Why 15%? Defaults are rare, so 85% accuracy is easy and says little. Missing a defaulter costs more than a second look at a safe applicant, so the line sits at 15% instead of 50%: it catches 41.8% of real defaulters, and about one in four flagged applicants really defaults.

    How the breakdown works: every tree is walked for this applicant and each split's change in expected log-odds is credited to the feature it used (Saabas path attribution). The starting point plus the contributions add up exactly to the model's output.

    Limits: a learning project, not a lending tool. Inputs not in the form are fixed at typical values from the training data. The data includes gender, which a real lender would need to review for fair-lending reasons.
